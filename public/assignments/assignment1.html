Assignment 1:
Allometric Scaling

How does a biological quantity change as an organism gets larger?

Before you begin

An allometric model relates a measured biological quantity B — such as metabolic rate, heart rate, or limb length — to body mass M. These models are important because they reveal how biological form and function scale across organisms of vastly different sizes, from a shrew to a whale, and help us predict unmeasured quantities, understand energy budgets, and connect structure to function. In this assignment you will fit, compare, and differentiate such models using the tools from class.

Mathematically, an allometric law can be expressed as:

B(M) = aMb,   M > 0.

Here a sets the scale and b is the allometric exponent.

Problem 4

A published study of California horn sharks reports allometric exponents of approximately 0.966 for resting metabolic rate and 1.073 for maximum metabolic rate. Gill area has exponent approximately 0.564 below 0.203 kg and approximately 1.012 above 0.203 kg. Treat these reported exponents as fixed values for this exercise; the measured relationships also have uncertainty.

  1. Let resting demand be R = rM0.966, maximum demand be Q = qM1.073, and gill area be G = gMp, with positive constants. Derive the mass exponents of G/R and G/Q for each reported gill-area regime. That is, for each regime, find the exponent e such that G/R ∝ Me and G/Q ∝ Me.
  2. For each regime, state whether each ratio rises or falls as mass increases. For a tenfold increase in mass within each regime, calculate the multiplicative change in G/Q.
  3. Using Python, plot G/R and G/Q versus M on a log-log plot from M = 0.01 kg to M = 10 kg, using the appropriate gill-area exponent for each mass. Assume r = q = g = 1 for the plot (since the constants cancel in the ratio). Describe what happens at the 0.203 kg breakpoint.
  4. Explain one reason that comparing fitted exponents alone cannot establish that gill area physically limits metabolic rate. In particular, consider uncertainty and what a surface-area argument assumes about biological structure.

Problem 5

Suppose metabolic power Y provides maintenance and growth:

Y = YcN + Ec dN/dt.

Here N is cell number, Yc is maintenance power per cell, Ec is the energy needed to make one cell, and each cell has mass Mc. Assume positive constants and Y = aM3/4 with M = McN.

  1. Eliminate N to show that dM/dt = (aMc/Ec)M3/4 − (Yc/Ec)M. Check units of both terms.
  2. Set dM/dt = 0 to derive the positive equilibrium mass Mmax. Explain the direction of growth just below and just above this mass.
  3. For initial mass 0 < M(0) = M0 < Mmax, substitute u = M1/4. Derive a linear differential equation for u, solve it, and transform back to an explicit formula for M(t). Verify the initial value and limiting mass.
  4. Choose numerical values for the constants so that Mmax = 10 kg and Yc/Ec = 0.5 per unit time. (For example, let Yc/Ec = 0.5 and solve for the remaining combination.) Solve the differential equation numerically using scipy.integrate.solve_ivp or a simple Euler method for three initial masses: M0 = 0.1, 1.0, and 5.0 kg. Plot M(t)/Mmax against dimensionless time τ = Yct/(4Ec) for each initial condition on the same axes. Compare their shapes. Does the model predict finite-time arrival at Mmax?
  5. Compare your numerical solutions with the analytical formula from part (c). Do they agree? Plot both for one initial condition to check.
